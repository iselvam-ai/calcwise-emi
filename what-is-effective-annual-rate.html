What is the effective annual rate?

Last updated: 25 September 2026

The stated interest rate is only part of what a loan costs. Processing fees and GST on those fees reduce the amount you actually receive, while you still repay the full EMI. The effective annual rate is the rate that reflects that gap.

Worked example

Take a ₹10,00,000 loan at 8.5% a year for 10 years, with a 2% processing fee and 18% GST on the fee:

  • Processing fee: 2% of ₹10,00,000 = ₹20,000
  • GST on the fee: 18% of ₹20,000 = ₹3,600
  • Amount you actually receive: ₹10,00,000 − ₹23,600 = ₹9,76,400
  • EMI: still about ₹12,399 a month, because it is based on the full ₹10,00,000 at 8.5%

You repay as if you borrowed ₹10,00,000, but receive ₹9,76,400. Solving for the rate that makes the same EMI pay off ₹9,76,400 gives an effective annual rate of about 9.06%, higher than the stated 8.5%.

Why it matters

Two loans with the same stated rate can cost different amounts once fees are added. A loan with a lower rate but a high fee can end up more expensive than one with a slightly higher rate and no fee. Comparing the effective annual rate puts both offers on the same basis.

Enter the fee, the GST and both rates into the Loan Comparison Calculator. It shows the effective annual rate for each offer, alongside the EMI and total cost.
