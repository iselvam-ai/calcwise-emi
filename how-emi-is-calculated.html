How EMI is calculated

Last updated: 25 September 2026

An equated monthly instalment (EMI) is the fixed amount you pay each month to repay a loan. Each payment covers the interest for that month and the rest reduces the principal. The EMI stays the same for the whole tenure on a fixed-rate reducing-balance loan.

The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P is the loan amount (principal).
  • r is the monthly interest rate: the annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. For 8.5% a year, r = 0.085 ÷ 12 ≈ 0.0070833.
  • n is the number of monthly instalments. For 10 years, n = 120.

Worked example

Take a ₹10,00,000 loan at 8.5% a year for 10 years (120 months):

  • Monthly EMI: about ₹12,399
  • Total paid over 120 months: about ₹14,87,828
  • Total interest: about ₹4,87,828
  • First EMI: about ₹7,083 interest and ₹5,315 principal
  • Last EMI: about ₹87 interest and ₹12,311 principal

The first instalment is mostly interest because interest is charged on the full outstanding balance. As the balance falls, each instalment pays off more principal. This is why prepaying early saves more interest than prepaying near the end.

What changes the EMI

  • A higher rate raises the EMI and the total interest.
  • A longer tenure lowers the EMI but raises the total interest, because the balance stays outstanding for longer.
  • A larger loan raises the EMI in proportion.

Note

This is an illustration of the standard formula. Your lender's EMI may differ because of processing fees, rounding, rate resets or a different calculation method. Check the figures in your loan agreement. See the Disclaimer.
