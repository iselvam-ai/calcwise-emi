Under the hood

How EMI Is Calculated

For reducing-balance loans, the EMI is usually calculated with the standard annuity formula:

EMI formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

P Principal
The loan amount.
r Monthly rate
Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100.
n Months
Number of monthly EMIs.

Each month, interest is charged on the outstanding balance; the rest of the EMI reduces the principal. Displayed amounts are rounded to the nearest rupee. Lenders may round the EMI differently, charge broken-period interest for the first month, or adjust the final EMI.

Frequently asked questions

Loan Basics

A prepayment reduces the outstanding principal. Because interest is charged on the reducing balance, later interest amounts fall, which either shortens the tenure or lowers the EMI, depending on what you choose with your lender. Prepayment charges depend on the loan type, lender and applicable regulations.

A floating rate is linked to a benchmark and can go up or down during the loan, which changes your EMI or tenure. A fixed rate stays the same for the period agreed with the lender. Fixed-rate terms and pricing vary by lender, so compare the full terms before choosing.

Lenders usually consider your credit score along with income, loan amount and other factors when deciding the interest rate they offer. The exact effect differs by lender and changes over time.

Home loan borrowers may be able to claim deductions for principal repayment under Section 80C and for interest under Section 24(b), subject to limits and conditions. Eligibility depends on the applicable Income Tax rules, tax regime, property type, loan purpose and individual circumstances. Tax information is provided for general reference only.
