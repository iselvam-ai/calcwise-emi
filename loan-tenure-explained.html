Loan tenure: shorter or longer?

Last updated: 25 September 2026

The tenure is the number of years you take to repay a loan. A longer tenure lowers the EMI, but it also means you pay interest for longer. A shorter tenure does the opposite.

Worked example

Here is a ₹10,00,000 loan at 8.5% a year, with different tenures:

  • 5 years: EMI about ₹20,517. Total interest about ₹2,30,992.
  • 10 years: EMI about ₹12,399. Total interest about ₹4,87,828.
  • 15 years: EMI about ₹9,847. Total interest about ₹7,72,531.
  • 20 years: EMI about ₹8,678. Total interest about ₹10,82,776.

Going from 5 to 20 years cuts the EMI by more than half, but the total interest is more than four times higher. The lower EMI is affordable only because you pay for longer.

How to choose a tenure

  • Pick the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford. Test whether the EMI still leaves room for savings and emergencies.
  • Avoid stretching the tenure just to fit a higher loan amount. A longer loan can look affordable while costing much more overall.
  • Remember that you can prepay later. Choosing a longer tenure gives flexibility, but you can shorten the loan with prepayments if your income grows. Check for prepayment charges first.

Note

These figures use the standard reducing-balance formula and are for illustration only. Lender EMIs can differ because of fees, rounding or rate changes. See the Disclaimer.
